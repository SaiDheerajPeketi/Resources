## Mental model

<Analogy title="A rehearsal for failure">

Threat modeling is a design rehearsal. The team walks an attacker through the real system before the attacker does it in production. The output is not the diagram; it is a set of better design decisions and testable security requirements.

</Analogy>

## Diagram what changes trust

Include:

- external actors and identities,
- processes and services,
- data stores and sensitive data classes,
- network or administrative boundaries,
- third parties and asynchronous channels,
- authentication and authorization transitions,
- secrets and key-management dependencies.

A trust boundary is where the level or owner of trust changes. A line between two boxes is not automatically a boundary, and a Kubernetes namespace is not automatically a security boundary.

## STRIDE as a prompt

| Category | Question | Typical control families |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spoofing | Can an actor impersonate another identity? | strong authentication, binding, anti-replay |
| Tampering | Can data or code change without detection? | authorization, integrity, signatures, immutability |
| Repudiation | Can actions be denied without evidence? | attributable audit events, time integrity, retention |
| Information disclosure | Can sensitive data cross an unintended boundary? | minimization, encryption, access control, redaction |
| Denial of service | Can scarce resources be exhausted? | quotas, isolation, backpressure, degradation |
| Elevation of privilege | Can a lower privilege gain a higher one? | least privilege, policy boundaries, sandboxing |

<KeyIdea><p>STRIDE generates candidates. Risk comes from attacker capability, preconditions, exposure, impact, existing controls, and the confidence of your evidence.</p></KeyIdea>

## Convert threats into work

Weak mitigation: “Use encryption.”

Testable mitigation: “Reset tokens are 128 bits or more of CSPRNG output, stored as digests, single-use, expire within 20 minutes, are invalidated on successful reset, and are never written to logs. Automated tests verify replay and expiry.”

Each material threat needs an owner and one disposition: mitigate, avoid, transfer, or explicitly accept. Accepted risk records scope, rationale, expiry/review date, and decision owner.

<Pitfalls>

- Drawing only infrastructure while omitting identities and data.
- Brainstorming a long threat list without prioritization or owners.
- Using a scoring formula to create fake precision from unsupported numbers.
- Listing controls that do not address the stated abuse path.
- Treating “internal network,” namespace, VPC, or VPN as inherently trusted.
- Running the exercise once and never updating it after architecture changes.
- Logging secrets or sensitive payloads in the name of repudiation controls.

</Pitfalls>

### Workshop sequence

Scope → objectives and assets → data-flow diagram → trust boundaries → abuse cases → risk → decisions → verification

### Ask at every boundary

- Who controls this input, identity, or dependency?
- What sensitive data crosses the boundary and where can it be copied?
- What happens if the dependency lies, delays, repeats, or disappears?
- Which control blocks the exact abuse path, and what test proves it?
- Who owns the remaining risk and when will the decision be reviewed?

### Remember

STRIDE helps generate threat candidates; it does not prioritize them or prove a mitigation works.

## Interview case

<InterviewPrompt title="Password-reset flow" difficulty="Medium" timebox="15 min">

Threat-model a password-reset flow that sends an emailed token and changes credentials through a public API. Identify the most important trust boundaries and abuse cases.

<Hint>Follow data from an unauthenticated client through the email provider and back. Consider token lifecycle, enumeration, replay, and existing sessions.</Hint>

<Solution>

Boundaries include the public client/API, identity store, reset-token store or signer, email provider, and the user’s mailbox. Threats include account enumeration, token guessing/leakage/replay, reuse after reset, CSRF, rate-limit evasion, mailbox compromise, host-header poisoning in links, and retained sessions. Mitigate with consistent responses, rate and abuse controls, opaque high-entropy single-use expiring tokens, trusted link construction, digest storage, session-revocation policy, and attributable audit events without token logging.

**Rubric:** real boundaries; token lifecycle; enumeration and replay; mitigations mapped to threats; verification ideas.

</Solution>

</InterviewPrompt>
