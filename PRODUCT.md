# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js App Router with TypeScript, local MDX content, static export, an installable offline-first PWA, and browser-local persistence. The application has no runtime server, account system, or cloud synchronization.

## Users

The primary user is a software professional with roughly one year of experience who has worked across domains, has forgotten or not yet learned portions of the interview syllabus, and is preparing for entry-to-mid-level roles while building enough depth to attempt more demanding interviews.

The role families are AI/ML engineering, data science, data analysis, data engineering, software engineering, frontend, backend, full-stack, Java, Python, Go, Android, iOS, cross-platform mobile, QA/SDET, DBA, DevOps/platform/SRE, cloud and solutions architecture, cybersecurity, fintech engineering, and quant/trading.

## Product Purpose

Interview Atlas is a self-contained technical interview reference and practice system. It turns a sprawling syllabus into a navigable role atlas and Stack Workbench where a learner can locate a concept or technology, understand it from first principles, study worked examples and commands, practice interview questions and DSA progressions, and save material into revision and review workflows.

Success means the learner can move between role maps, technologies, shared prerequisites, DSA sheets, role plans, and public company-process guides without losing context; use the core curriculum without leaving the app; prepare offline; and quickly switch between deep study, practice, diagnostics, adaptive review, and last-minute revision.

## Positioning

The product's distinctive mechanism is a paired navigation model: the cross-role skill graph traces prerequisites and downstream systems, while the Stack Workbench organizes languages, frameworks, databases, mobile, data/AI, testing, build, and operations ecosystems as layered technology stacks. The corpus is exhaustive against versioned coverage manifests rather than presented as an unstructured collection of links.

## Operating Context

- The product opens directly into the atlas; diagnostics and generated plans are optional and never block access.
- The primary workflow is browse or search, select a topic, technology, problem, sheet, role, or company guide, learn and practice, then optionally save it to a named revision list or local review queue.
- C++17-compatible examples are primary for DSA and systems interviews, with C++20/23 callouts. Python is used for AI/data work; SQL, Bash, YAML, Terraform/HCL, and TypeScript appear where their domains require them.
- The hub targets Indian and global product companies, startups, fintechs, quant firms, and infrastructure companies.
- AWS, Azure, and GCP receive equal cloud coverage, with explicit OCI terminology and service comparisons.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static, searchable topic library with role, level, interview-style, and status filters.
- Interactive prerequisite maps with an accessible outline equivalent.
- Stack Workbench technology discovery with ecosystem index, layered map, and contextual inspector.
- Full-depth technology manuals. Every one of the 115 published languages, frameworks, databases, mobile platforms, AI/data systems, build tools, and operations technologies progresses from basic to expert; covers at least eight subject-specific focus areas; and teaches internals, lifecycle, state, concurrency, testing, diagnostics, performance, security, compatibility, and recovery. Every theory section pairs a plain-English explanation with a bounded analogy and concrete example. Each manual also includes five or more worked examples, misconception corrections, twelve graded interview questions with scoring rubrics, twelve flashcards, a printable A–Z focus map, and revision checkpoints. A published manual may not carry overview status.
- Full-depth learning roadmaps. All six domains and fifteen role paths have at least five ordered stages with explicit prerequisites, concepts, deliberate practice, linked resources, a portfolio deliverable, readiness gates, interview prompts, common misconceptions or failure modes, capstone evidence, mock-loop expectations, and a final revision checklist. Technology ladders also pair every basic-to-expert stage with a worked-example exercise, proof gate, and interview check.
- Beginner-first teaching is mandatory across every technology, domain, and role roadmap. The first explanation must say what the subject is, why it matters, give a familiar but bounded analogy, offer one small first exercise, and define three essential terms. Exact technical scope follows through progressive disclosure instead of appearing before the learner has a mental model.
- Nested Atlas 75, Atlas 180, and Atlas 300 DSA sheets with themed filters, stable progress, language-switchable artifacts, attributed topic-only crosswalks, and a per-problem external practice destination. Problems 1–90 now satisfy the full-depth contract: every lesson has an original exact statement, worked trace, bounded analogy, baseline and optimal approaches, invariant proof, complexity derivation, traps, follow-ups, and reviewed C++17, Java 17, Python, and strict TypeScript reference implementations. This includes the complete Atlas 75 plus matrix, bitwise, and trie extensions toward Atlas 180. Later lessons must say “guided overview” until their reviewed solutions and exact lesson contract are complete. Use a direct LeetCode match when one is confidently mapped; otherwise provide a targeted GeeksforGeeks implementation search without pretending it is an exact match.
- Every role-track field note has exactly four clear top-level reading sections: **Full Notes**, **Cheat Sheet**, **Interview Questions**, and **Revision Material**. Full Notes preserves the basic-to-advanced teaching path—concept map and outcomes, bounded analogy, exact mechanisms and trade-offs, worked example, failure modes, misconceptions, and authoritative sources—while the other three sections provide purpose-built study views without duplicating the entire lesson on screen.
- Every generated field note is a complete interview chapter, not an overview. It exposes the full subtopic syllabus; connects every chapter to plain language, exact mechanisms, a worked example, a failure mode, and a mastery check; includes normal-path, boundary, debugging, comparison, and teach-back scenarios; provides a definition-to-advanced question ladder; and finishes with at least eight flashcards and twelve closed-book revision prompts. The six original hand-authored notes may exceed this structure, but no generated note may fall below it.
- Field-note depth must come from teaching content rather than repeated scaffolding. Every generated note begins at zero assumptions, defines at least eight relevant terms, gives a concrete example for each definition, states the nearest confusion or boundary, and then preserves the intermediate mechanism and advanced trade-off. A short expert sentence may not be relabelled as a beginner explanation without unpacking its vocabulary.
- Optional diagnostics, prerequisite-aware role plans, and explainable local FSRS-style review. Topic confidence remains explicitly controlled by the learner.
- Date-stamped public-process company guides and fifteen role-path maps; no proprietary interview dumps.
- Original lessons, diagrams, examples, questions, solutions, rubrics, flashcards, cheatsheets, and printable revision sheets.
- Revision lists, notes, bookmarks, progress states, attempts, diagnostic results, plans, review states, and versioned JSON backup/restore stored locally.
- Downloadable offline track, ecosystem, technology, DSA-sheet, role, and full-library packs with quota checks and versioned integrity metadata.
- Optional local owner passphrase gate with session unlock and local cooldown. It is a casual device gate, not authentication or protection for the public GitHub Pages files.
- No embedded compiler, hosted judge, certification dumps, pirated material, verbatim proprietary question banks, backend, secure authentication, or cloud sync.
- Regulatory, financial, security, and other time-sensitive content is date-stamped and educational, not professional advice.

## Brand Commitments

- Working name: Interview Atlas.
- Voice: precise, calm, direct, encouraging, and technically rigorous without sounding academic for its own sake.
- User-pinned visual world: Technical Field Atlas.
- The visual system is a light technical manual with cool paper, dark ink, blueprint blue, restrained amber and teal annotations, tabbed indexes, ruled panels, and diagram-led navigation.
- Avoid generic course dashboards, card grids, streaks, points, badges, visual hype, and decorative gamification.

## Evidence on Hand

No existing lessons, brand assets, testimonials, commercial claims, or user data are available. Demonstration progress and attempts must be clearly synthetic. Sources used for the corpus must be recorded per topic.

## Product Principles

1. Map before memorizing: always show where a concept sits and why it matters.
2. Teach, test, and revise in one place: no topic is complete without all three modes.
3. Depth is layered: intuition first, interview fluency second, advanced trade-offs last.
4. Shared foundations stay shared: do not duplicate the same concept across role silos.
5. Offline and user-owned by default: content and personal study data remain usable without an account.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All core actions must work by keyboard, focus must remain visible, maps require a semantic outline alternative, status cannot rely on color alone, layouts must reflow at 400% zoom, and motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.
